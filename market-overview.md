# Market Overview

## Kabad Company --- Recycling & Recyclable Materials Research

**Status:** Early-stage research and validation\
**Initial focus:** Plastic scrap and recycled materials\
**Initial geography:** Delhi-NCR and surrounding industrial markets\
**Last updated:** September 2026

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Executive Summary

India has a large and increasingly formalized recycling ecosystem, but
the movement of recyclable materials from fragmented supply to
industrial demand remains operationally difficult.

Kabad Company is researching this gap from the ground up.

The project is not beginning with the assumption that a marketplace or
software platform is the answer. Instead, we are studying actual
transactions between suppliers, recyclers, processors, and industrial
buyers to understand where value is lost and where better coordination
could create measurable improvements.

The initial research focuses on plastic because it combines large
volumes, multiple material grades, established recycling infrastructure,
recurring industrial demand, and significant variation in quality,
location, processing requirements, and transportation economics.

The central research question is:

> **Can better information, coordination, verification, and eventually
> technology make recyclable-material sourcing more reliable and
> economically viable for both suppliers and industrial buyers?**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. The Recycling Ecosystem

The recycling value chain is not a single industry. It is a network of
participants with different roles.

A simplified flow is:

``` text
Waste Generation
      ↓
Collection
      ↓
Aggregation / Scrap Dealers
      ↓
Sorting
      ↓
Processing
      ↓
Recycling
      ↓
Recycled Material / Granules
      ↓
Industrial Manufacturers
      ↓
New Products
```

Between these stages are additional activities such as transportation,
quality inspection, weighing, documentation, payment, storage, and
compliance.

A transaction can fail at any one of these points.

For example:

-   The material may be available but not match the buyer's required
    grade.
-   The supplier's price may be acceptable before freight but unworkable
    after freight.
-   The buyer may require a recurring quantity that one supplier cannot
    provide.
-   Contamination or moisture may result in deductions.
-   Payment terms may not work for the supplier.
-   The supplier and buyer may have incomplete information about each
    other.
-   A buyer requirement may become outdated before supply is located.

This makes the problem more complex than simply "finding a buyer for
scrap."

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. Why Plastic Is the Initial Focus

Plastic is the first material category being studied because it contains
multiple commercially important streams and has an established ecosystem
of collectors, scrap dealers, processors, recyclers, granule
manufacturers, and plastic-product manufacturers.

Initial material categories include:

-   PP --- Polypropylene
-   HDPE --- High-Density Polyethylene
-   LDPE --- Low-Density Polyethylene
-   PET --- Polyethylene Terephthalate
-   ABS
-   HIPS
-   Mixed plastics
-   Other recyclable plastic streams

These materials can differ significantly in value depending on:

-   Polymer type
-   Grade
-   Color
-   Form
-   Source
-   Cleanliness
-   Moisture
-   Contamination
-   Sorting quality
-   Processing stage
-   Quantity
-   Location
-   Transportation
-   Buyer specifications

Therefore, a simple category such as "PP scrap" is often insufficient to
determine an executable transaction price.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. The Supply-Side Problem

A significant portion of recyclable-material supply is fragmented among
small and medium-sized collectors, scrap dealers, aggregators, and local
networks.

A supplier may know:

> "I have 10 tonnes of PP."

But an industrial buyer may need to know:

-   What exact PP grade?
-   Rigid or flexible?
-   Natural, mixed, or colored?
-   Washed or unwashed?
-   Regrind, flakes, lumps, or another form?
-   What is the contamination level?
-   Where is it located?
-   How quickly can it be dispatched?
-   What quantity is available now?
-   Is the supply recurring?
-   What payment terms are expected?

The difference between a basic description and a commercially useful
specification can determine whether a transaction happens.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. The Buyer-Side Problem

Industrial buyers and recyclers often have recurring material
requirements.

A requirement may look like:

``` text
Material: PP
Quantity: 20 MT
Quality: Defined buyer specification
Location: Delhi-NCR
Frequency: Monthly
Price basis: Delivered
```

But finding suitable supply requires more than discovering a supplier.

The buyer needs confidence in:

-   Material quality
-   Quantity
-   Supplier reliability
-   Pricing
-   Logistics
-   Delivery timing
-   Documentation
-   Payment terms
-   Rejection and deduction conditions

This creates an opportunity to improve the information layer around the
transaction.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. Price Discovery

One of the most important research areas for Kabad Company is price
discovery.

A material does not necessarily have one universal market price.

The executable value can depend on:

``` text
Material specification
        +
Quality
        +
Quantity
        +
Supplier location
        +
Buyer location
        +
Freight
        +
Processing requirements
        +
Payment terms
        +
Market conditions
        =
Executable transaction economics
```

A supplier may reject a buyer's price because the supplier's local
alternative is better.

A buyer may reject a supplier's price because transportation or quality
deductions make the delivered economics unattractive.

Therefore, the project aims to collect transaction-level information
rather than relying only on broad market price claims.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. Logistics Is Part of the Product

For low-value or high-volume materials, transportation can materially
change transaction economics.

A useful sourcing workflow therefore needs to consider:

-   Pickup location
-   Delivery location
-   Quantity
-   Vehicle type
-   Freight estimate
-   Loading requirements
-   Unloading requirements
-   Distance
-   Delivery timeline

A price that appears attractive at the supplier location may not remain
attractive after transportation.

This is why Kabad Company's research treats **material + price +
location + logistics** as one commercial problem.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 8. Regulation and Formalization

India's recycling ecosystem is increasingly influenced by formal
environmental and compliance frameworks.

Plastic waste management and Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) have
increased the importance of documented recycling and processing
pathways.

Government and industry data also indicate a growing formal network of
producers, importers, brand owners, and plastic waste processors.

For Kabad Company, regulation is not being treated as the entire
business opportunity. Instead, it is one factor contributing to the
broader formalization of the recycling ecosystem.

The project will continue tracking:

-   Plastic Waste Management Rules
-   EPR requirements
-   Registered plastic waste processors
-   Recycling and processing capacity
-   Traceability requirements
-   Compliance-related demand

Regulatory information will be verified against current government and
authoritative sources before being used for commercial decisions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 9. Market Fragmentation as a Research Opportunity

The ecosystem contains participants with different levels of technology
adoption.

Some businesses may operate through:

-   Phone calls
-   WhatsApp
-   Personal relationships
-   Local brokers
-   Informal price discovery
-   Spreadsheet records

Others may have:

-   ERP systems
-   Procurement systems
-   Digital documentation
-   Compliance systems
-   Structured supplier networks

This creates a possible technology opportunity, but it also creates a
constraint:

> **A useful product must fit the way participants actually work.**

Kabad Company therefore intends to study existing workflows before
deciding what software should be built.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 10. Our Field-First Approach

Instead of starting with a large technology platform, we are following
this sequence:

``` text
Research
   ↓
Talk to Buyers
   ↓
Understand Requirements
   ↓
Talk to Suppliers
   ↓
Attempt Real Transactions
   ↓
Record Outcomes
   ↓
Identify Repeated Problems
   ↓
Design Better Processes
   ↓
Automate Where Useful
   ↓
Build Technology
```

This approach allows the project to distinguish between problems that
genuinely need software and problems that can be solved through better
processes or relationships.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 11. What We Measure

For each opportunity or transaction, we aim to capture non-confidential
operational information such as:

  Category           Examples
  ------------------ -----------------------------------------------
  Material           PP, HDPE, PET, LDPE
  Form               Scrap, flakes, regrind, granules
  Quantity           Tonnes / truckload
  Quality            Grade, contamination, moisture
  Location           Supplier and buyer region
  Price              Buying indication / offered price
  Freight            Estimated logistics cost
  Payment            Advance / credit / other terms
  Status             Sourced / rejected / negotiated / completed
  Failure reason     Price / quality / freight / quantity / timing
  Repeat potential   One-time / recurring

The objective is to understand what makes a transaction executable and
repeatable.

## 12. Potential Technology Applications

As the dataset and operational understanding grow, we will explore AI
and software applications such as:

### Requirement Structuring

Convert unstructured buyer messages into structured requirements.

Example:

``` text
"Need around 20 ton PP regrind in NCR, regular requirement"
```

could become:

``` text
Material: PP
Form: Regrind
Quantity: ~20 MT
Location: NCR
Frequency: Recurring
```

### Supplier Matching

Compare a buyer requirement against available supply based on:

-   Material
-   Grade
-   Quantity
-   Location
-   Price
-   Quality
-   Availability

### Research Automation

Use AI to accelerate:

-   Company research
-   Buyer discovery
-   Supplier research
-   Market intelligence
-   Document analysis

### Operational Knowledge

Build an internal knowledge base covering:

-   Materials
-   Buyers
-   Suppliers
-   Transaction outcomes
-   Logistics patterns
-   Common quality issues

## 13. What We Are Not Assuming Yet

The project deliberately avoids making several assumptions at the
validation stage.

We are not assuming that:

-   A marketplace is automatically the right business model.
-   Suppliers will consistently accept buyer-indicated prices.
-   Every material category has sufficient liquidity.
-   Software alone will solve trust problems.
-   Logistics can always be outsourced without affecting economics.
-   A transaction can be repeated simply because one transaction worked.
-   Broad market-size estimates translate directly into our addressable
    opportunity.

These are questions to be tested through real market activity.

## 15. Research Principles

Kabad Company follows a few principles during validation:

### 1. Real transactions over theoretical assumptions

Market behavior is more useful than assumptions about what participants
should do.

### 2. Transparent commercial terms

Material, quality, transportation responsibility, payment terms, and
other important conditions should be clear to participants.

### 3. No fabricated traction

We will distinguish between research, active experiments, completed
transactions, and future plans.

### 4. Protect confidential information

Private commercial information and personal contact data will not be
published in this repository.

### 5. Technology follows the problem

We will build software where repeated evidence shows that technology can
improve the workflow.

## 17. Conclusion

The opportunity being explored by Kabad Company is not simply "selling
scrap."

It is the broader challenge of making recyclable materials easier to
discover, evaluate, move, and connect with industrial demand.

We are starting with a narrow material category and geography, learning
directly from market participants, documenting the workflow, and using
technology only where it can improve the economics or reliability of the
process.

The objective is to move from:

**fragmented information → better coordination → repeatable transactions
→ useful data → scalable infrastructure.**
