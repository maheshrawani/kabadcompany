# Buyer Requirement Workflow

## Kabad Company — From Buyer Need to Actionable Material Requirement

**Status:** Early-stage operating framework  
**Purpose:** Create a repeatable process for turning an unstructured buyer request into a commercially useful sourcing requirement.

---

## 1. Objective

A buyer may communicate a requirement in a short message:

> "Need 20 ton PP in NCR."

This is not yet a complete sourcing requirement.

The objective of this workflow is to convert the message into enough structured information to determine:

- What material is needed
- Whether the material is available
- Whether the supplier can meet the specification
- Whether the price can work
- Whether transportation works
- Whether the transaction can be repeated

The workflow should remain simple enough to use through phone calls and WhatsApp.

---

## 2. Core Principle

Do not start supplier sourcing before understanding the buyer requirement.

The sequence should generally be:

```text
Buyer Requirement
      ↓
Requirement Qualification
      ↓
Commercial Basis
      ↓
Supplier Search
      ↓
Material Verification
      ↓
Logistics Check
      ↓
Buyer Review
      ↓
Transaction
```

---

## 3. Step 1 — Capture the Original Requirement

Record the buyer's original words before interpreting them.

Example:

```text
"Need around 20 ton PP regrind in NCR.
Regular requirement."
```

This preserves the original context.

Then convert it into structured information.

---

## 4. Step 2 — Identify the Material

Ask:

- What material?
- What grade?
- What form?
- What application?
- Is the material post-industrial or post-consumer?
- Is mixed material acceptable?

For example:

```text
Material: PP
Form: Regrind
Grade: To be confirmed
Source: To be confirmed
```

Do not assume a grade simply because the buyer used a broad material name.

---

## 5. Step 3 — Understand Quality

Ask the buyer what quality conditions matter.

Potential fields:

- Color
- Moisture
- Contamination
- Odor
- Polymer purity
- Washing
- Particle size
- Melt flow requirement, where applicable
- Additives/fillers, where applicable
- Previous application/source

Not every field is required for every material.

The objective is to capture the quality characteristics that affect acceptance.

---

## 6. Step 4 — Quantity

Capture:

- Immediate quantity
- Minimum shipment quantity
- Maximum acceptable quantity
- Monthly requirement
- Expected frequency

Example:

```text
Immediate requirement: 20 MT
Monthly requirement: 40–60 MT
Frequency: Recurring
```

This distinction helps separate a one-time opportunity from a potentially repeatable demand stream.

---

## 7. Step 5 — Delivery Location

Capture the commercial delivery location.

At minimum:

```text
City:
Industrial Area:
State:
```

For freight estimation, more detailed information may be required internally.

Do not publish private addresses in the public repository.

---

## 8. Step 6 — Price Basis

Never record only a number.

Record:

```text
Price:
Currency:
Unit:
Price basis:
Quality basis:
Taxes/charges:
Validity:
```

Examples:

```text
₹X/kg delivered to buyer location
```

or

```text
₹X/kg ex-supplier location
```

A price without its basis should be treated as incomplete.

---

## 9. Step 7 — Payment Terms

Ask:

- Advance?
- On delivery?
- Credit?
- How many days?
- Is payment linked to quality approval?
- Are deductions made after inspection?

Payment terms can affect the supplier's willingness to accept a transaction even when the price appears attractive.

---

## 10. Step 8 — Trial and Sample Process

Determine whether the buyer requires:

- Photos
- Video
- Physical sample
- Laboratory test
- Trial load
- Full-load approval

Example:

```text
Photos → Sample → Trial quantity → Recurring supply
```

The actual process should follow the buyer's existing quality system where possible.

---

## 11. Step 9 — Determine Requirement Status

Each requirement should have a status.

Suggested statuses:

```text
New
↓
Qualification Pending
↓
Qualified
↓
Supplier Search
↓
Material Found
↓
Buyer Review
↓
Sample / Trial
↓
Negotiation
↓
Transaction
↓
Recurring
```

Alternative outcomes:

```text
Rejected
Expired
On Hold
No Supplier
Commercially Unviable
```

---

## 12. Step 10 — Determine Urgency

Record when the buyer actually needs the material.

Examples:

- Immediate
- Within 48 hours
- This week
- This month
- Recurring
- No fixed timeline

An old requirement should not be treated as active simply because it remains in a database.

---

## 13. Step 11 — Supplier Search

Only after qualification should supplier sourcing begin.

Search based on:

- Material
- Grade
- Form
- Quality
- Quantity
- Location
- Price
- Availability
- Recurring capability

Supplier discovery can use:

- Existing network
- Direct outreach
- Industry directories
- Recycling facilities
- Scrap dealers
- Referrals
- Other verified market channels

---

## 14. Step 12 — Supplier Response Format

To reduce back-and-forth, supplier responses should ideally follow a structured format:

```text
Material:
Grade:
Form:
Quantity available:
Location:
Quality:
Photos:
Sample:
Price:
Price basis:
Availability:
Recurring supply:
Payment expectation:
```

This makes multiple supplier responses easier to compare.

---

## 15. Step 13 — Commercial Feasibility

A potential match should be checked before being presented as a transaction opportunity.

Basic logic:

```text
Supplier Price
      +
Estimated Freight
      +
Other Known Costs
      =
Estimated Delivered Cost
```

Then compare with:

```text
Buyer Workable Price
```

The difference is not automatically profit.

Other factors may include:

- Quality risk
- Payment risk
- Rejection risk
- Brokerage/service fee
- Loading/unloading
- Working capital
- Taxes
- Other transaction costs

---

## 16. Step 14 — Present the Material to the Buyer

The buyer should receive enough information to make an informed decision.

Example:

```text
Material: PP Regrind
Quantity: 20 MT
Location: Ghaziabad
Quality: Supplier specification attached
Photos: Available
Sample: Available
Price: ₹X/kg delivered
Availability: Immediate
Transport: Included in delivered price
```

Do not describe material as verified if it has not actually been verified.

---

## 17. Step 15 — Verification

Verification can occur at different levels.

### Level 1 — Supplier declaration

Information provided by supplier.

### Level 2 — Photos/video

Visual evidence.

### Level 3 — Sample

Physical sample reviewed by buyer.

### Level 4 — Inspection

Physical inspection or third-party verification.

### Level 5 — Successful transaction

Material has successfully passed the buyer's commercial and quality process.

The verification level should be recorded.

---

## 18. Step 16 — Logistics

Before final confirmation, determine:

- Pickup point
- Delivery point
- Quantity
- Vehicle requirement
- Loading
- Unloading
- Freight
- Delivery timeline

A transaction should not be considered commercially confirmed until logistics are reasonably understood.

---

## 19. Step 17 — Transaction Confirmation

Before dispatch, confirm:

```text
Material
Grade
Quantity
Quality basis
Price
Price basis
Freight responsibility
Payment terms
Delivery location
Dispatch date
Quality inspection process
Rejection/deduction rules
```

Both parties should understand the commercial basis.

---

## 20. Step 18 — Post-Transaction Recording

After delivery, record:

- Actual quantity
- Accepted quantity
- Rejected quantity
- Quality issue
- Final price
- Freight
- Payment status
- Delivery performance
- Buyer feedback
- Supplier feedback
- Whether repeat demand exists

This is where the transaction becomes useful data.

---

## 21. Failed Transaction Analysis

A failed transaction should not simply be deleted.

Record why it failed.

Suggested categories:

```text
Price
Quality
Quantity
Freight
Timing
Payment
Trust
Supplier reliability
Buyer changed requirement
Material unavailable
Specification mismatch
Other
```

Repeated failure categories can reveal where the business needs improvement.

---

## 22. Example End-to-End Workflow

### Buyer Message

> "Need 15 MT HDPE regrind in Ghaziabad."

### Qualification

```text
Material: HDPE
Form: Regrind
Quantity: 15 MT
Location: Ghaziabad
Grade: Pending
Color: Pending
Quality: Pending
Price basis: Pending
Timing: Immediate
Frequency: Pending
```

### Follow-up

The buyer provides:

```text
Grade: Buyer-specific
Color: Mixed
Quantity: 15 MT
Delivery: Ghaziabad
Sample: Required
Payment: Buyer-defined
Frequency: Monthly
```

### Supplier Search

Potential suppliers are contacted with the structured requirement.

### Supplier Response

```text
HDPE regrind
Quantity: 18 MT
Location: Noida
Photos: Available
Sample: Available
Price: ₹X/kg
Transport: To be estimated
```

### Feasibility

Freight and other known costs are calculated.

### Buyer Review

Supplier material is presented with the available evidence.

### Trial

Sample or trial load is evaluated.

### Transaction

Commercial terms are confirmed.

### Post-Transaction

Actual results are recorded.

### Repeat

If the material performs successfully and the buyer still has demand, recurring supply can be explored.

## 24. Future AI Workflow

Once enough real examples exist, AI could assist with:

### Requirement Extraction

Convert natural-language messages into structured fields.

### Missing Information Detection

Identify what the buyer has not specified.

### Supplier Search

Suggest relevant suppliers based on the requirement.

### Match Scoring

Compare material, quality, quantity, location, price, and timing.

### Conversation Summarization

Turn WhatsApp or call notes into structured records.

### Follow-Up

Identify requirements that need confirmation or have become stale.

### Transaction Learning

Analyze successful and failed transactions to identify recurring patterns.

AI should assist human decisions rather than inventing missing commercial information.

---

## 25. Data Quality Rules

The future system should distinguish between:

- Confirmed
- Supplier-reported
- Buyer-reported
- Estimated
- Unverified
- Expired

For example:

```text
Price: ₹X/kg
Source: Supplier
Status: Supplier-reported
Verified: No
```

This is more useful than treating every data point as fact.

---

## 26. Operating Principles

### Be transparent

Important commercial conditions should not be hidden from participants.

### Do not guarantee unverified material

A supplier's statement is not the same as an inspection result.

### Do not guarantee price without buyer confirmation

Market indications can change.

### Do not treat old requirements as active

Every requirement needs a verification date.

### Record failures

Failed transactions contain valuable market information.

### Start simple

The process should work through phone calls, WhatsApp, spreadsheets, and basic tools before requiring software.

---

## 27. Success Metrics

During early validation, useful metrics include:

- Qualified buyer requirements
- Active requirements
- Supplier responses
- Potential matches
- Samples requested
- Trials completed
- Transactions completed
- Repeat transactions
- Average transaction quantity
- Failed transaction rate
- Failure reasons
- Time from requirement to supplier match
- Time from match to transaction
- Recurring demand identified

These metrics are intended to measure operational learning, not vanity growth.

## 28. Current Status

This workflow is a working operating framework.

It will be updated as Kabad Company conducts more buyer conversations, supplier searches, and real-world transactions.

The goal is to turn an informal sourcing activity into a repeatable process that can eventually be supported by software and AI.
