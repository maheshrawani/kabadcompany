# Supplier Onboarding & Verification

## Kabad Company — Supplier Network Operating Framework

**Status:** Early-stage operating framework  
**Initial focus:** Scrap dealers, collectors, aggregators, recyclers and other sources of recyclable plastic materials

---

## 1. Purpose

The supplier network is one of the most important parts of Kabad Company's initial validation.

The objective is not simply to collect a large list of scrap dealers.

The objective is to understand:

- What materials each supplier can provide
- How much they can provide
- Where the material is located
- What quality information is available
- How reliable the supply is
- What price the supplier expects
- Whether supply can become recurring
- Which buyer requirements the supplier can realistically satisfy

The network should therefore prioritize **usable supply information over contact-count growth**.

---

## 2. Who Can Be a Supplier?

Potential supplier categories include:

- Scrap dealers
- Scrap aggregators
- Waste collectors
- Plastic collection businesses
- Industrial scrap generators
- Plastic processors
- Recycling facilities
- Manufacturing units with production scrap
- Material traders
- Other verified sources of recyclable materials

A supplier does not necessarily need to be a traditional scrap dealer.

A manufacturing facility generating 20 tonnes of clean production scrap may be a highly relevant source.

## 5. Initial Supplier Conversation

The first objective is to understand the supplier's actual supply rather than immediately negotiate a deal.

Useful questions include:

### Material

1. What material do you currently have?
2. What grade is it?
3. What form is it in?
4. Is it sorted?
5. Is it washed?
6. What is the source of the material?

### Quantity

7. How much is available now?
8. What quantity can you supply per truck?
9. Can you supply this material regularly?
10. What is your typical monthly volume?

### Quality

11. What is the contamination level?
12. Is there moisture?
13. Is the material mixed?
14. Are photos or videos available?
15. Can a sample be provided?

### Commercial

16. What price do you expect?
17. Is the price pickup or delivered?
18. What payment terms do you require?
19. How quickly can the material be dispatched?

The goal is to establish whether the supplier can actually satisfy industrial requirements.

## 7. Supplier Availability

Availability should be time-bound.

Avoid recording:

> "Supplier has 50 MT PP."

Instead record:

```text
Available quantity: 50 MT
Verified on: YYYY-MM-DD
Expected dispatch: Within 3 days
```

This reduces the risk of presenting stale supply.

## 9. Verification Levels

Supplier information can be classified by verification level.

### Level 0 — Unverified

Information received but not independently checked.

### Level 1 — Basic Verification

Company/location/contact has been checked.

### Level 2 — Material Evidence

Photos or videos received.

### Level 3 — Physical Sample

Sample available or inspected.

### Level 4 — Buyer Approval

Buyer has accepted the material/specification.

### Level 5 — Successful Transaction

Supplier has successfully completed a transaction.

### Level 6 — Repeat Supply

Supplier has completed multiple successful transactions.

The verification level should be stored with the supplier record.

Useful evidence may include:

- Photos
- Videos
- Weighbridge slips
- Packing photos
- Previous test reports where applicable
- Sample
- Physical inspection
- Buyer feedback

Evidence should never be presented as independently verified unless it has actually been verified.

## 11. Supplier Reliability

A supplier's reliability should be learned from behavior over time.

Potential metrics include:

- Response time
- Accuracy of quantity
- Accuracy of material description
- Quality consistency
- Dispatch reliability
- Delivery reliability
- Pricing stability
- Payment compliance
- Repeat transaction performance

The objective is not to create arbitrary scores too early.

The objective is to collect enough evidence to understand reliability.

---

## 12. Price Communication

Kabad Company should distinguish between:

### Supplier expectation

What the supplier wants to receive.

### Buyer indication

What the buyer says they can pay.

### Market indication

A broader observed market range.

### Executable transaction

A price accepted by the relevant parties after considering quality, logistics and commercial terms.

This distinction is important because:

> A supplier asking for ₹X/kg does not mean a buyer will pay ₹X/kg.

Similarly:

> A buyer indicating ₹X/kg does not mean every supplier can deliver at ₹X/kg.

---

## 13. Transparent Commercial Communication

Important terms should not be hidden.

When presenting a requirement to suppliers, the communication should make clear:

- Material
- Quantity
- Buyer/location where appropriate
- Price basis
- Transportation responsibility
- Quality requirements
- Payment terms where confirmed
- Any inspection/rejection conditions

For example:

```text
BUYING REQUIREMENT

Material: PP Regrind
Quantity: 20 MT
Buying indication: ₹X/kg
Basis: Delivered to Ghaziabad
Transport: Supplier-arranged
Quality: Buyer specification
Sample: Required
Payment: As agreed after approval
```

If the buyer price is not confirmed, it should be described as an indication rather than a guaranteed price.

---

## 14. Transportation Responsibility

Transportation should be explicitly discussed.

Possible arrangements include:

### Supplier arranged

Supplier handles transportation to the buyer.

### Buyer arranged

Buyer arranges pickup.

### Third-party logistics

A separate logistics provider is used.

### Coordinated

Kabad Company helps coordinate logistics without owning the vehicle.

The initial model should avoid taking unnecessary inventory or vehicle ownership risk.

---

## 15. Supplier Matching

A supplier should only be considered a potential match if the material reasonably aligns with the buyer requirement.

Key dimensions:

```text
Material
+
Grade
+
Form
+
Quality
+
Quantity
+
Location
+
Price
+
Availability
+
Timing
=
Potential Match
```

A potential match is not the same as a confirmed transaction.

---

## 16. Supplier Bypass Risk

In any multi-party sourcing network, participants may attempt to transact directly after being introduced.

This is a commercial reality rather than simply a technical problem.

Kabad Company can reduce unnecessary bypass risk by creating value through:

- Consistent buyer demand
- Supplier access to multiple requirements
- Qualification
- Transaction coordination
- Market information
- Documentation
- Logistics coordination
- Reliable settlement processes
- Repeat business

The objective should be to make the network useful enough that participants prefer continuing through it.

---

## 17. Supplier Communication Channels

Early-stage operations can use:

- Direct WhatsApp conversations
- WhatsApp broadcast lists
- Carefully managed groups
- Phone calls
- Email
- Spreadsheets
- Internal CRM/database

A large public group is not automatically the best option because it can expose participants to unnecessary contact and reduce control over commercial communication.

Channel choice should be tested with actual suppliers.

## 22. Failed Supplier Match

If a supplier cannot satisfy a requirement, record the reason.

Examples:

- Price too high
- Quantity insufficient
- Quality mismatch
- Material unavailable
- Freight too expensive
- Supplier changed price
- Dispatch delay
- Payment disagreement
- Buyer specification changed
- Supplier communication issue

Patterns across these failures can improve future sourcing.

---

## 23. Supplier Data Quality

Every important data point should ideally have:

```text
Value
Source
Date
Verification status
```

Example:

```text
Quantity: 20 MT
Source: Supplier
Verified: 2026-09-20
Status: Supplier-reported
```

This helps prevent old or unverified information from being treated as current fact.

## 24. AI Opportunities

As the supplier network grows, AI could assist with:

### Supplier Profile Creation

Convert unstructured WhatsApp messages into structured records.

### Material Extraction

Identify:

- Material
- Grade
- Form
- Quantity
- Location
- Price

### Missing Information

Identify what still needs to be asked.

### Matching

Compare supplier inventory with active buyer requirements.

### Follow-Up

Identify stale availability information that needs reconfirmation.

### Supplier Research

Help discover and research potential suppliers.

### Transaction Summaries

Turn conversations into concise commercial records.

AI should never invent material specifications, prices, availability, or verification status.

---

## 26. Supplier Network Principles

### Start with relevant suppliers

A smaller qualified network is more useful than a large inactive database.

### Keep information current

Availability expires.

### Be transparent

Do not misrepresent buyer prices or transaction status.

### Protect private information

Supplier contact data should remain private.

### Verify progressively

Do not create unnecessary friction before there is a real business opportunity.

### Reward reliability

Repeat suppliers should become easier to work with because their transaction history provides evidence of reliability.
